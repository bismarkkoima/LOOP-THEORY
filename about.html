The name

A loop is the simplest thing you can make from a length of metal and the hardest to make well. Close it badly and you have a gap that catches on a jumper. Close it properly and the join disappears.

That join is the whole test, and it is the one thing a photograph will not show you. So it became the name, and then the mark — two loops, offset, overlapping.

How the pieces are made

We do not own a workshop. We work with a maker network — small studios and independent jewellers who already do one thing extremely well, rather than one factory doing everything adequately.

A piece is made after you order it, in the size you asked for. That is why the site says two to four weeks rather than next-day, and it is not a thing we intend to fix. Nothing is warehoused, nothing is discounted to clear, and nothing is destroyed unsold at the end of a season.

What gets checked

Every listing is hand-checked for finish and plating quality before it earns a spot on the site — and a few do not earn one. What we look at:

  • The join. Closed flush, filed back, no catch under a fingernail.
  • The plating. Even across edges and inside curves, where thin plating shows first.
  • The clasp. Worked one-handed, twenty times, because that is how it will be worn.
  • The weight. Hollow where hollow is right, solid where it is not. A hoop should be forgettable by evening.

What things cost

There is no department store markup in these prices, and no invented original price to discount from. Where a piece shows a previous price, that is a price it was genuinely sold at.

What is left is the cost of making it, the cost of getting it to you, and a margin thin enough that we can keep sourcing pieces worth wearing. Small-batch pricing is not a slogan here; it is the only reason the numbers work.

What we are not

Not a fine jeweller. Nothing here is an heirloom, and we would not sell it to you as one. These are pieces to wear daily, at prices that do not make daily wear a worry — brass that darkens, plating that eventually goes, steel that outlives both.

The FAQ is honest about what each finish does over time. Read it before you buy, not after.
